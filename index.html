Why sell-through rate isn't on this tab

Gildings' scraped sale results show ~99–100% "sold" in every year — almost certainly because their site removes or hides a lot's page once it's unsold, rather than leaving it visible the way yours does. Comparing that number against your own real, independently-verified 82.7% sell-through would overstate the gap and isn't a fair comparison, so it's deliberately left out here. Average hammer price is used instead — it's calculated the same way on both sides and isn't affected by that difference.

What this shows, and what it doesn't yet

Gildings consistently hammers at a higher average price than you, every year in the comparison — a real, consistent gap, not a one-off. It doesn't yet say *why*: it could be a different item mix (higher-value antiques vs. general household), a different bidder base, or straightforward market positioning. The semantic/category comparison and topic modelling work in progress is aimed at answering that "why" question next.

What this would need to become real

The lot-matching mechanism above already works on your real sale history — what's still missing is your actual bidder registration/interest data from Easy Live: who's registered, what categories they've bid on before, how recently. With that, the "est. interested bidders" column in the table above stops being a simulation and this becomes a real gap-finder: genuine under-served categories worth sourcing more of, and over-supplied ones where cataloguing effort might be better spent elsewhere. Worth noting honestly: on sell-through alone, your categories are all fairly healthy (80–88%) — a real bidder audit is more likely to sharpen *where* to focus than to reveal a hidden crisis.

What this needs to become the real thing

Two steps: run this over the full corpus rather than a 10% sample (cheap — same pipeline, just more embedding calls), and cross-reference the resulting Sutton/Gildings topics directly — e.g. is "fine art paintings" a real Gildings category with little Sutton equivalent, or does it just show up more because Gildings catalogues differently? That comparison is what actually explains the ~3.4× average-price gap on the Market Position tab, not just the pricing gap on its own.

06 · Draft description assistant

From a rough description to a grounded first draft

Cataloguing hundreds of lots by hand is a real bottleneck — this is a first look at using your own 6 years of descriptions to draft new ones, grounded in genuinely comparable past lots rather than a generic guess.

Real retrieval, dummy generation

What this needs to become real

The retrieval half already works on real data — nearest-neighbour search over embedded past descriptions. What's templated here is the "draft" text itself: a real version would pass the retrieved comparables to a language model to actually write a draft in your cataloguer's style, not just quote the closest match. The retrieval infrastructure (embeddings + similarity search) is the expensive part and it's already built — adding real generation on top is comparatively small.
